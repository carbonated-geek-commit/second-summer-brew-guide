Style

What you're brewing

BJCP 25A — a moderate-strength golden ale: subtle fruity-spicy Belgian yeast complexity over grainy, slightly sweet Pilsner malt. A little upfront sweetness is fine; a good one always finishes dry and balanced. High carbonation and medium body give it a slightly creamy mouthfeel.

Expect esters of lemon, orange, grapefruit, and pear with light pepper/clove phenols — all of it from malt and fermentation, never from added fruit or spice. Yeast character stays subtler here than in other Belgian styles; the Pilsner malt matters just as much.

Style guardrails: no caramel/crystal malt — caramel flavor is out of style; winning recipes reach for toasted malts (aromatic) instead. Keep hops noble and restrained: BU:GU ratio 0.25–0.5, sweet spot ~0.3–0.4; this recipe lands at ~0.43.

By the numbers — 25 award-winning recipes

Mean Brews' dataset: 2 best-of-show, 11 gold, 4 silver, 5 bronze, 3 other awards.

  • OG averaged 1.064 and is trending down — this recipe brews at 1.060, right where winners are heading.
  • IBU ranged 18–34, average 26 — exactly ours. Aroma-hop additions are rising over time; the style is getting more hop-forward.
  • Pilsner malt appears in 100% of recipes (avg ~75% of grist); wheat malt in half (avg 10%); cane sugar in 42% (avg 6.5%). Our grist mirrors all three.
  • The Chimay strain (WLP500 / Wyeast 1214) is the most-used yeast and gaining on the Duvel strain year over year.
  • Average winning mash pH 5.37; average carbonation 3.2 volumes — high carbonation is structural to the style.

Sources: BYO — Belgian Blond: Style Profile (Jamil Zainasheff) · Mean Brews — Belgian Blond Ale All-Grain Recipe

Brew day

Red IPA — brew day

~72% mash efficiency · 60-min boil · 4.75 gal into fermenter. BIAB: 7 gal full-volume, squeeze, no sparge.

  1. Treat liquor: 1 tsp gypsum + ½ tsp CaCl₂ in the mash; ½ Campden tab if chlorinated tap.
  2. Strike 4.4 gal @ 165°F → dough in.
  3. Mash at 152°F, stir at 30 min.60 min
  4. Batch sparge 3.5 gal @ 170°F; collect 6.4 gal.
  5. CheckpointPre-boil gravity ~1.048. Low? Extend the boil slightly or accept a touch less ABV.
  6. Boil. Bittering: 2 oz Cascade @ 60 — or swap in 1 oz Columbus.60 min
  7. 1 oz Cascade @ 15.15 min
  8. Whirlfloc + ½ tsp yeast nutrient @ 10.10 min
  9. 1 oz Cascade @ 5.5 min
  10. Flameout → cool to 170°F → whirlpool 2 oz Cascade.20 min
  11. Chill to ≤ 65°F; 4.75 gal into the fermenter.
  12. Checkpoint — OG gateOG 1.065 ±.003. Record it before pitching.
  13. Pitch 2 × US-05 at ≤ 65°F — or 1 pack Lutra kveik at 75–90°F.

Brew day

Belgian Blonde — brew day

~72% mash efficiency · 75-min boil · 4.75 gal into fermenter. BIAB: 7.2 gal full-volume. Targets: mash pH ~5.4, calcium ~50 ppm, balanced sulfate/chloride. The long boil isn't optional — it drives off DMS precursors from the lightly kilned Pilsner malt.

  1. Treat liquor: ½ tsp CaCl₂ only — soft water, no gypsum.
  2. Strike 3.5 gal @ 160°F → dough in. Sugar stays out of the mash.
  3. Mash at 149°F.60 min
  4. Sparge 4.3 gal @ 170°F; collect 6.7 gal.
  5. CheckpointPre-boil gravity ~1.038 — before sugar.
  6. Boil. 1 oz Styrian Goldings @ 60 (~16 IBU).75 min
  7. 1 oz Saaz @ 15.15 min
  8. 12 oz cane sugar into the boil @ 10 — never the mash. Whirlfloc + nutrient @ 10.10 min
  9. 1 oz Saaz @ flameout.
  10. Chill to 65°F; 4.75 gal into the fermenter.
  11. Checkpoint — OG gateOG 1.060 ±.003. Record it before pitching.
  12. Pitch WLP500 / Wyeast 1214 — 2 pouches, or 1 + a 1.5 L starter — at 65°F.

Fermentation

Red IPA — fermentation

  1. Hold 64–66°F.days 1–4
  2. Dry hop day 4: 2 oz Cascade (+ optional 1 oz Centennial).day 4
  3. Free rise 68–70°F to finish.
  4. Checkpoint — FG gateFG 1.012–1.014, stable — two matching readings two days apart (days 10 & 12). Nothing moves forward until this passes.
Summer alternative: 1 pack Omega Lutra kveik @ 75–90°F, dry hop day 2, FG by ~day 5. Same FG gate applies: two matching readings two days apart.

Fermentation

Belgian Blonde — fermentation

  1. Hold 65–66°F.days 1–3
  2. Free rise to 73°F — let the esters build.days 3–10
  3. Checkpoint — FG gateFG 1.008–1.010, stable — two matching readings two days apart (days 10 & 14).
Pitch-rate note: this style is pitch-sensitive — err low, never high. A restrained pitch drives the ester/phenol profile (Jamil targets 0.75M cells/mL/°P; Mean Brews goes as light as a 1 L starter). The Chimay strain wants a mid-60s °F start — that's the 65°F pitch and free rise to 73°F above. Duvel-strain recipes start in the low 70s instead.
Dry-finish rescue: if attenuation stalls, next time hold the cane sugar out of the boil and add it to the fermenter once primary slows — make the yeast finish dinner before dessert. Full attenuation of the simple sugars, not a lower mash temp, is what makes this beer finish dry.

Packaging

Belgian Blonde — packaging

Heavy glass only at style carbonation. The 3.2-volume target belongs in pressure-rated glass — Belgian 750 cork-and-cage, champagne, heavy swing-top. Standard longneck bottles are not rated for 3.2 volumes of CO₂ — never put this carbonation level in them. In standard bottles, prime to ~2.7 volumes instead.
  1. Style carbonation: 3.2 volumes CO₂ = 6.3 oz corn sugar / 4.75 gal — pressure-rated glass only; condition 3 weeks @ 70–75°F.3 wk
  2. Standard bottles: 5 oz corn sugar → ~2.7 volumes.3 wk
  3. Keg: ~30 psi @ 38°F for 10 days, then vent to serving pressure.10 d
